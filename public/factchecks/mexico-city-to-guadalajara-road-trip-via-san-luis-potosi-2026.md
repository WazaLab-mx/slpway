## DETAILED FINDINGS

### CLAIM 1: Via SLP total drive ~730 km / 8h 30m

**CLAIM:** "Via San Luis Potosí (Hwy 57D + 80/90): ~730 km / 8h 30m of driving, split into 2 days."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Segment distances sum to roughly that figure: CDMX→SLP about 402–412 km and SLP→GDL about 327–331 km (about 729–743 km combined). Independent drive-time estimates for the two legs (about 4–5 hours plus about 3.5–5 hours) support a total pure-driving window near 8.5 hours under normal conditions.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Tarifas CAPUFE — CDMX to San Luis Potosí route (412 km, updated 2026)](https://tarifascapufe.com.mx/ruta-de-ciudad-de-mexico-a-san-luis-potosi/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Tarifas CAPUFE — San Luis Potosí to Guadalajara route (331 km, updated 2026)](https://tarifascapufe.com.mx/ruta-de-san-luis-potosi-a-guadalajara/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Mejores Rutas — SLP → Guadalajara ~328 km](https://mx.mejoresrutas.com/distancias/san-luis-potos%C3%AD/guadalajara-jal-mx/)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
The article’s ~730 km total is a fair rounded sum of the two toll-highway legs. The 8h 30m figure is a reasonable mid-range driving-only estimate; real elapsed time varies with CDMX exit traffic, stops, and works. No material contradiction was found.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Multiple independent distance calculators converge within ~15 km.

### CLAIM 2: Direct Hwy 15D ~540 km / 6h 30m

**CLAIM:** "Direct route (Hwy 15D): ~540 km / 6h 30m."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Distance is essentially correct (~547–552 km on the Autopista de Occidente / 15D corridor). Typical published drive times are closer to 5.5–6 hours in clear conditions; 6h 30m is plausible with traffic or stops but reads high as a baseline “direct” figure.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Tarifas CAPUFE — Guadalajara to Ciudad de México (547 km, updated 2026)](https://tarifascapufe.com.mx/ruta-de-guadalajara-a-ciudad-de-mexico/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Escapada H — CDMX–Guadalajara ~551.5 km, ~5.5 hours (Sept 2026)](https://www.escapadah.com/destinos/2026/9/12/cuanto-es-de-casetas-y-gasolina-para-ir-a-guadalajara-el-15-de-septiembre-45697.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [El Informador — 15D ~550 km, ~5.5–6 hours (May 2026)](https://www.informador.mx/jalisco/esta-es-la-mejor-ruta-para-viajar-desde-jalisco-a-cdmx-en-tiempo-record-segun-la-ia-20260501-0080.html)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Highway 15D as the fast direct option is correct. The ~540 km rounding is fine. The ~2-hour “detour premium” versus the article’s 8h 30m via-SLP total still roughly holds if the direct baseline is ~6–6.5 hours, but cleaner sources put unconstrained direct driving nearer 5.5–6 hours.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Distance well corroborated; time is the soft part of the claim.

### CLAIM 3: Direct-route tolls ~600 MXN each way

**CLAIM:** Comparison table and framing: direct Hwy 15D tolls "~600 MXN" each way for a passenger car.

**VERDICT:** ❌ FALSE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
2026 route-level caseta totals for CDMX↔Guadalajara on the 15D corridor are about 1,315–1,481 MXN for a standard automobile—more than double the article’s ~600 MXN figure. No current CAPUFE-linked calculator supports a ~600 MXN one-way total for that full trip.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Tarifas CAPUFE — GDL–CDMX peajes total 1,315.52 MXN (2026)](https://tarifascapufe.com.mx/ruta-de-guadalajara-a-ciudad-de-mexico/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Escapada H — 11 casetas, ~1,481 MXN one way (Sept 2026)](https://www.escapadah.com/destinos/2026/9/12/cuanto-es-de-casetas-y-gasolina-para-ir-a-guadalajara-el-15-de-septiembre-45697.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [El Informador — ~1,315 MXN peajes on 15D (May 2026)](https://www.informador.mx/jalisco/esta-es-la-mejor-ruta-para-viajar-desde-jalisco-a-cdmx-en-tiempo-record-segun-la-ia-20260501-0080.html)
- **Official tariff reference:** [CAPUFE — Tarifas vigentes 2026 (PDF)](https://pot.capufe.mx/gobmx/transparencia/Doc/TransparenciaF/Tarifas/Vigentes/2026/Tarifas-vigentes-2026.pdf)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
The ~600 MXN figure appears to reflect an outdated or incomplete booth set, not the full 2026 passenger-car path. Because the article presents ~600 MXN as the comparable “each way” toll for the direct route, the claim is contradicted by current aggregated caseta schedules. Exact cash totals still vary slightly by entry ramps and TAG discounts; inventing a single official “total” beyond published route sums would be inappropriate, but none of the checked 2026 sources land near 600 MXN.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Multiple independent 2026 route tallies cluster near 1.3–1.5k MXN.

### CLAIM 4: Via-SLP tolls ~700–800 MXN each way

**CLAIM:** "Tolls: ~700–800 MXN each way for the full CDMX–SLP–GDL route" (FAQ also equates this to about $40–46 USD); comparison table lists "~750 MXN."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Adding current CDMX→SLP (~442 MXN on one common Mexiquense+57D path) and SLP→GDL (~570 MXN on 49D/80D) yields about 1,012 MXN one way—higher than the article’s 700–800 / ~750 band. The via-SLP route is still a real toll corridor in the right order of magnitude, but the published total understates recent caseta sums and the table’s implication that via-SLP tolls exceed direct-route tolls is inverted versus 2026 figures.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Tarifas CAPUFE — CDMX–SLP peajes 442 MXN](https://tarifascapufe.com.mx/ruta-de-ciudad-de-mexico-a-san-luis-potosi/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Tarifas CAPUFE — SLP–GDL peajes 570 MXN](https://tarifascapufe.com.mx/ruta-de-san-luis-potosi-a-guadalajara/)
- **Official tariff reference:** [CAPUFE — Tarifas vigentes 2026 (PDF)](https://pot.capufe.mx/gobmx/transparencia/Doc/TransparenciaF/Tarifas/Vigentes/2026/Tarifas-vigentes-2026.pdf)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Booth lists on the CAPUFE-linked calculator are route-dependent (Circuito Exterior Mexiquense vs classic Tepotzotlán/Polotitlán stacks change the CDMX exit total). Across the checked 2026 paths, a full CDMX–SLP–GDL one-way toll bill near 1,000 MXN is more defensible than 700–800 MXN. The article correctly notes cash or TAG payment; it does not provide a sourced booth-by-booth total. Relative to Claim 3, via-SLP looks cheaper than direct 15D in current data (~1.0k vs ~1.3k+), opposite the article table’s ~750 vs ~600 framing.

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium — Strong on understatement; exact peso total depends on chosen CDMX approach roads.

### CLAIM 5: Via route uses Hwy 57D then Hwy 80/90

**CLAIM:** "Via SLP (Hwy 57D + 80/90)" and Day 3 "Leave SLP via Hwy 80 west."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
The standard toll path north from CDMX toward SLP is México–Querétaro / Libramiento Noreste on federal 57D. SLP to Guadalajara primarily uses federal 80 / 80D (Lagos de Moreno–Zapotlanejo), with a short 90D approach into the Guadalajara metro—matching the article’s “80/90” shorthand. Federal 45 is not the main spine of this itinerary.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Wikipedia — Carretera Federal 80D (SLP–Lagos–Zapotlanejo)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carretera_Federal_80D)
- **Corroborating Source:** [CalcularRuta — SLP to Guadalajara turn-by-turn (80D then 90D)](https://www.calcularruta.com/de-san-luis-potosi-a-guadalajara.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [CalcularRuta — CDMX to SLP via MEX-57D](https://www.calcularruta.com/de-ciudad-de-mexico-a-san-luis-potosi.html)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Highway numbering in the article is accurate for traveler guidance. Day-1 “Periférico Norte to Hwy 57D” is a normal CDMX exit pattern. No contradiction with SICT/CAPUFE route naming was found for the core legs.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Consistent across routing guides and federal highway designations.

### CLAIM 6: SLP and Jalisco both US State Department Level 2 (like France or the UK)

**CLAIM:** "Both SLP and Jalisco are at US State Department Travel Advisory Level 2 — same as France or the UK."

**VERDICT:** ❌ FALSE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
As of the May 29, 2026 Mexico advisory (still the operative state-by-state page at verification), San Luis Potosí is Level 2 (Exercise Increased Caution), but Jalisco is Level 3 (Reconsider Travel) for terrorism, crime, and kidnapping. France and the UK remain Level 2, so the France/UK comparison fits SLP only—not Jalisco.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [U.S. Department of State — Mexico Travel Advisory (issued May 29, 2026)](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Border Report — May 29, 2026 advisory summary listing Jalisco at Level 3](https://www.borderreport.com/regions/mexico/us-updates-travel-advisory-for-mexico-over-risk-of-crime-terrorism-kidnapping/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [U.S. Department of State — France Travel Advisory (Level 2)](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/france.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [U.S. Embassy UK — UK advisory renewed Level 2 (Sept 9, 2026)](https://uk.usembassy.gov/travel-advisory-united-kingdom-of-great-britain-and-northern-ireland-september-2026/)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
The article’s daytime toll-highway caution is still sound practical advice, and Guadalajara metro travel is carved out as unrestricted for U.S. government employees even under Jalisco Level 3. Equating the entire state of Jalisco to France/UK Level 2 is not supported by the current official map. SLP Level 2 is correct.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Direct read of the State Department country advisory page.

### CLAIM 7: SLP → Guadalajara via Hwy 80 arrives metro in about 3.5 hours

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Fast-route estimates for SLP→Guadalajara cluster around 3h 35m–3h 53m for ~327–331 km on 80/80D. A 9:00–12:30 window is therefore realistic without major delays; longer published times (~4–5 hours) reflect slower routing or traffic, not a contradiction of the article’s optimistic schedule.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [CalcularRuta — 330 km, 3h 53 min](https://www.calcularruta.com/de-san-luis-potosi-a-guadalajara.html)

Tlaquepaque as a short highway diversion on the Guadalajara approach is geographically sensible. 2026 maintenance reports on parts of 80D can add delay, but the article does not claim a roadwork-free guarantee—only a schedule that matches clear-traffic drive times.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Multiple Google-distance-class sources agree on sub-4-hour best cases.

### CLAIM 8: San Luis Potosí has a UNESCO Centro Histórico

**CLAIM:** The detour unlocks "SLP’s UNESCO Centro Histórico" / "UNESCO World Heritage Centro Histórico."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
SLP’s historic centre is a nationally declared Zona de Monumentos Históricos (1990) and is included in UNESCO’s 2010 serial property Camino Real de Tierra Adentro (component associated with the historic urban fabric). It is not a separate, stand-alone UNESCO “Historic Centre of San Luis Potosí” inscription in the same sense as Querétaro’s Historic Monuments Zone. Calling it simply “UNESCO Centro Histórico” is therefore directionally right but imprecise.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [UNESCO — Camino Real de Tierra Adentro (WHC 1351)](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1351/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [INAH — San Luis Potosí historic monuments zone](https://lugares.inah.gob.mx/en/node/4813)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Wikipedia — Centro histórico de San Luis Potosí (Camino Real component)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_hist%C3%B3rico_de_San_Luis_Potos%C3%AD)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
For travelers, the UNESCO association is real and meaningful. For strict heritage classification, the article overstates by implying a city-centre listing equivalent to Querétaro’s dedicated inscription (which the same itinerary correctly flags as UNESCO).

**CONFIDENCE:** High — UNESCO and INAH primary pages agree on the Camino Real / national-zone distinction.

## PATTERN ANALYSIS

### ACCURACY PATTERNS
Driving distances, highway numbers (57D, 80/90), and segment times are the strongest parts of the piece. Money and safety-policy figures are weaker: tolls are systematically understated, and the Jalisco advisory level is wrong.

### BIAS INDICATORS
The guide sells the SLP detour as obviously “worth it.” That editorial stance is fine, but the toll comparison table currently makes the direct route look cheaper on casetas than it is, which softens a real cost of the 15D option and slightly muddies the tradeoff analysis.

### SOURCING QUALITY
No booth-level CAPUFE citation or State Department link appears in the article text. Distance claims behave like Google-Maps-class estimates; toll and advisory claims needed primary 2026 sources the post did not surface.
