## DETAILED FINDINGS

### CLAIM 1: Mexican World Cup 2026 host cities

**CLAIM:** "If you are visiting Mexico for the 2026 tournament, the host cities are not your only option… Hotel rates in Mexico City, Guadalajara, and Monterrey jumped…"

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
FIFA and U.S. Soccer confirm Mexico’s three 2026 host cities as Mexico City, Guadalajara, and Monterrey. By September 2026 the tournament window (June–July) had already run; match listings on FIFA’s host-city pages show games at Mexico City Stadium, Estadio Guadalajara, and Estadio Monterrey. No other Mexican city hosted World Cup matches.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [FIFA Announces 16 Cities — U.S. Soccer (Mexico: Guadalajara, Mexico City, Monterrey)](https://ussoccer.com/stories/0001/01/fifa-announces-16-cities-to-host-2026-fifa-world-cup-across-the-usa-mexico-and-canada-app)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Mexico City | FIFA World Cup 2026™ Host City](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/canadamexicousa2026/mexico/mexico-city)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Guadalajara | FIFA World Cup 2026™ Host City](https://www.fifa.com/en/tournaments/mens/worldcup/canadamexicousa2026/mexico/guadalajara)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
The article correctly treats CDMX, Guadalajara, and Monterrey as the Mexican hosts and frames the other ten cities as non-hosts. That framing matches the official 16-city list (11 U.S., 3 Mexico, 2 Canada). No contradiction found.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Official organizer records agree.

### CLAIM 2: Host-city hotel rates up 173%–333%

**CLAIM:** "Hotel rates in Mexico City, Guadalajara, and Monterrey jumped 173%–333% for the tournament window." / "Average hotel rates +173% to +333%"

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
AMVITUR, citing Lighthouse data in April 2026, reported year-over-year room-rate surges of 333% in Guadalajara, 218% in Monterrey, and 173% in Mexico City — exactly the article’s 173%–333% band. Lighthouse’s own World Cup pricing commentary repeats the same three figures.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Mexican Hotel Rates Outpace North American Peers Amid World Cup — Mexico Business News (22 Apr 2026)](https://mexicobusiness.news/infrastructure/news/mexican-hotel-rates-outpace-north-american-peers-amid-world-cup)
- **Corroborating Source:** [How to price your hotel for the World Cup — Lighthouse](https://www.mylighthouse.com/es/resources/blog/world-cup-2026-hotel-revenue-strategy)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
The percentages are YoY market averages for the tournament window, not every property or every night. Other Lighthouse snapshots (post-draw, game-day premiums) use different bases and different percentages; they do not rebut the AMVITUR YoY range the article cites.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Industry dataset cited by trade press and the data vendor.

### CLAIM 3: Near-stadium stays up to +2,000%

**CLAIM:** "near-stadium stays up to +2,000%" / "near-stadium properties up to +2,000%"

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Extreme spikes above 2,000% were documented for Mexico City match nights — notably ~2,373% at Le Méridien Mexico City Reforma ($157 → $3,882) in The Athletic’s December 2025 analysis. That hotel is central Reforma, not adjacent to Estadio Azteca/Banorte. “Near-stadium” overstates location even though the magnitude (“up to ~2,000%+”) is real for selected high-demand listings.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Hotel prices in World Cup host cities surge by more than 300% — The Athletic](https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/6875796/2025/12/10/hotel-prices-in-world-cup-host-cities-surge-by-more-than-300-after-schedule-confirmed/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Mexico City Hotel Rates Jump as Much as 2,373% — Engine](https://engine.com/blog/mexico-city-hotel-rates-jump-as-much-as-2-373-for-2026-world-cup-opener-early-data-shows)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Core numeric claim holds; geographic framing is loose. Average market YoY rises (Claim 2) and outlier listing spikes are different metrics; the article mixes them without clarifying that +2,000% is an extreme listing example, not the market average.

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium — Spike documented; “near-stadium” not verified for that example.

### CLAIM 4: Drive times from San Luis Potosí to the three hosts

**CLAIM:** "Roughly equidistant from all three Mexican host cities (4h to GDL, 4h 30m to CDMX, 5h 40m to MTY)."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
SLP is centrally located relative to the three hosts, but the three times are not equal, so “equidistant” is marketing language. Independent route calculators typically put SLP→Guadalajara around 4–5 hours, SLP→Mexico City closer to ~5 hours (often longer than 4h 30m), and SLP→Monterrey highly variable (highway ~500+ km; some tools show ~5.5–6h under free-flow, others much longer with congestion). The order of magnitude is right; the precise minutes and the “equidistant” wording oversimplify.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Distancia San Luis Potosí → Guadalajara — Mejores Rutas (~4h 53m, 328 km)](https://mx.mejoresrutas.com/distancias/san-luis-potos%C3%AD/guadalajara-jal-mx/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Cómo llegar de San Luis Potosí a Ciudad de México — Calcular Ruta (~5h, 419 km)](https://www.calcularruta.com/de-san-luis-potosi-a-ciudad-de.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Driving Time SLP to Guadalajara — TravelMath (~3h 56m estimate)](https://www.travelmath.com/driving-time/from/San+Luis+Potosi,+Mexico/to/Guadalajara,+Mexico)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Drive times depend on tolls, traffic, and stops. Treating 4h / 4h30 / 5h40 as fixed facts overstates precision. Useful as planning ballparks; weak as exact geography.

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium — Multiple routing sources; high variance by tool and conditions.

### CLAIM 5: San Luis Potosí metro population ~1.31M

**CLAIM:** "San Luis Potosí — San Luis Potosí, pop. ~1.31M metro"

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Official Metrópolis de México / INEGI 2020 figures put the Zona Metropolitana de San Luis Potosí at about 1,243,980 inhabitants. Data México’s alternate metro composition (including Zaragoza) reports ~1,271,366. Neither matches ~1.31M. The article’s tilde suggests approximation, but it sits ~5–7% above the best census metro totals without citing a post-2020 official estimate.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Anexo: Áreas metropolitanas de México — ZM San Luis Potosí 1,243,980 (2020)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anexo:%C3%81reas_metropolitanas_de_M%C3%A9xico)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Data México — San Luis Potosí metro profile (~1,271,366)](https://www.economia.gob.mx/datamexico/es/profile/geo/san-luis-potosi-992402)
- **Corroborating Source:** [INEGI Censo 2020 SLP press release (municipio capital 911,908)](https://www.inegi.org.mx/contenidos/saladeprensa/boletines/2021/EstSociodemo/ResultCenso2020_SLP.pdf)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Directionally correct (metro above one million). Exact ~1.31M is not supported by the 2020 official delimitation. Similar inflation appears for Puebla (~3.2M claimed vs ~2.78M official) and Querétaro (~1.59M vs ~1.53M); Mérida (~1.32M) and Guanajuato city (~195k) track census closely.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Census metro totals are clear; article figure is rounded high.

### CLAIM 6: Parque Tangamanga is a 411-hectare urban park

**CLAIM:** "UNESCO Centro Histórico (Camino Real de Tierra Adentro), 411-hectare urban park…"

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
CECURT, the state agency that runs Parque Tangamanga I, states the park’s total surface is 411 hectares (Tangamanga II is listed separately at 189 ha). UNESCO’s Camino Real de Tierra Adentro serial property includes the historic centre of San Luis Potosí as component 1351-039 — so the UNESCO half of the sentence is also correct (the 411 ha refers to the park, not the UNESCO polygon).

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [CECURT — ¿Quiénes somos? (411 hectáreas)](https://cecurt.slp.gob.mx/quienes-somos/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [UNESCO Camino Real de Tierra Adentro — maps (Historic centre of San Luis Potosí)](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1351/maps/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [El Exprés — Tangamanga I 41st anniversary (411 ha)](https://www.elexpres.com/nota.php?story_id=326523)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Some tourism pages say 420 ha; the operating agency’s figure is 411. Article matches the official operator.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — State park operator primary source.

### CLAIM 8: Real de Catorce — Pueblo Mágico, ~2,750 m, pop. ~1k

**CLAIM:** "Real de Catorce — San Luis Potosí, pop. ~1k… Pueblo Mágico in the high desert at 2,750 m elevation, ~3h from SLP."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Pueblo Mágico status since 2001 is correct. Elevation commonly cited is ~2,728 m (INEGI/local summaries cluster ~2,699–2,755 m); 2,750 m is a fair round. Population is not ~1,000: INEGI 2020 locality total is 1,395. Drive time from SLP city via Matehuala is often nearer 3–3.5+ hours depending on route and stops — the “~3h” is optimistic but not absurd.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Real de Catorce — Wikipedia / INEGI locality (1,395 hab.; ~2,728 m; Pueblo Mágico 2001)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_de_Catorce)
- **Corroborating Source:** [El Universal — Pueblo mágico a mayor altitud (2,728 m; 1,395 inhabitants)](https://www.eluniversal.com.mx/destinos/este-es-el-pueblo-magico-a-mayor-altitud-en-mexico/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Pueblos América — Real de Catorce población 1,395](https://mexico.pueblosamerica.com/i/real-de-catorce/)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Status and elevation band hold. “~1k” undercounts by ~40% versus the 2020 census locality figure.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Census and tourism ministry program record.

### CLAIM 9: Direct U.S. flights from SLP — Dallas and Houston

**CLAIM:** "direct flights from Dallas (Volaris/American) and Houston (United)."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
2026 schedule aggregators show nonstop SLP–DFW on American and Volaris, and SLP–IAH on United (and also Volaris). OMA/Volaris announced Dallas and Houston routes in 2025; United has long operated Houston. Airline mix matches the article (United for Houston; American and Volaris for Dallas), with Volaris also present on Houston.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [OMA — New international routes Dallas, Houston, San Antonio (Mar 2025)](https://news.oma.aero/news/oma-boosts-san-luis-potosis-air-connectivity-with-new-international-routes-67414-f8d04.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [AeroCorner SLP destinations (updated Sep 2026) — DFW AA/Volaris; IAH UA/Volaris](https://aerocorner.com/airports/slp/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [FlightRadar24 — United UA5284 SLP–IAH schedule samples 2026](https://www.flightradar24.com/data/flights/ua5284)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Claim is accurate for summer 2026 connectivity. Frequencies change seasonally; existence of the named nonstops is confirmed.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Airport operator announcement plus 2026 schedule data.

### CLAIM 10: Most listed cities are U.S. State Department Level 2 (like France or the UK)

**CLAIM:** "Are these cities safe? Most are at US State Department Travel Advisory Level 2 (same as France or the UK) as of early 2026."

**VERDICT:** 🔀 MISLEADING

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Mexico’s country-level advisory is Level 2 as of the 29 May 2026 update, and several listed destinations (e.g., Querétaro, San Luis Potosí) are Level 2. But the list includes Morelia in Michoacán — a Level 4 “Do Not Travel” state (with tightly limited U.S. government employee access to Morelia) — and Mérida in Yucatán, which is Level 1. Saying “most” are Level 2 while the article promotes Morelia as a summer base and equates the set to France/UK without state-level caveats misleads readers on risk differentiation.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Mexico Travel Advisory — Travel.State.gov (issued 29 May 2026)](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/mexico.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [US State Dept Updated Mexico Travel Advisory May 29, 2026 — Discover Mexico](https://discovermexico.info/news/2026-06-04-us-state-dept-mexico-advisory-update-may-2026/)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Country-level Level 2 and France/UK analogy can be fair in isolation. Applied to this specific 10-city list without noting Michoacán Level 4 and Yucatán Level 1, the FAQ oversimplifies in a way that matters for trip planning.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Direct advisory text for Michoacán, Yucatán, Querétaro, and SLP.

## PATTERN ANALYSIS

### ACCURACY PATTERNS
Tournament geography, headline hotel YoY percentages, Tangamanga size, T+L awards, and SLP–Texas nonstops are strong. Soft errors cluster in rounded metro populations, compressed drive times, and safety generalizations.

### BIAS INDICATORS
The piece advocates SLP as the #1 base for World Cup visitors — commercial framing for San Luis Way — but checkable host-city and rate claims are not fabricated to support that pitch. The main risk distortion is underplaying Michoacán’s Level 4 status while listing Morelia.

### SOURCING QUALITY
No inline citations in the article. Independent verification recovered Tier 1–2 sources (FIFA/U.S. Soccer, State Department, INEGI/CECURT, Lighthouse/AMVITUR, T+L) for most quantitative claims.
