## DETAILED FINDINGS

### CLAIM 1: Invented around 1919 by Cristina Jalomo in Soledad

**CLAIM:** "Invented around 1919 by doña Cristina Jalomo in Soledad de Graciano Sánchez."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
State and municipal narratives credit Cristina Jalomo (also styled Cristina Jalomo viuda de Medellín) of Soledad de Graciano Sánchez with creating the dish around 1919. Soledad marked a centennial in 2019; Decreto 0438 (2016) recognizes the enchilada potosina as intangible cultural heritage originating there and identifies Jalomo as the official inventora in the heritage story.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Wikipedia summary of Decreto 0438 / heritage narrative — Enchilada potosina](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enchilada_potosina)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Ayuntamiento de Soledad — FENAE 2019 / centenario attribution](http://www.municipiosoledad.gob.mx/node/525)
- **Corroborating Source:** [La Jornada San Luis — Soledad declared “Cuna de la Enchilada Potosina”](https://lajornadasanluis.com.mx/declaran-sgs-cuna-la-enchilada-potosina/)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Exact creation is oral tradition, not a dated kitchen log; a few municipal notes float 1920. “Around 1919” matches the dominant commemorative year used by Soledad and regional press. Attribution to Jalomo and Soledad is consistent across official heritage framing.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Official heritage + municipal centennial align.

### CLAIM 2: Cristina Jalomo lived 1874–1973

**CLAIM:** "Doña Cristina Jalomo (1874–1973) of Soledad de Graciano Sánchez."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
The 1874–1973 lifespan is the standard attribution in Wikipedia’s heritage-linked entry and in regional gastronomy write-ups that treat Jalomo as official inventora. No contradictory dates surfaced in press or municipal material reviewed.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Enchilada potosina — Cristina Jalomo (1874–1973)](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enchilada_potosina)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Ciudad San Luis — Enchiladas Potosinas (same dates)](https://www.ciudadsanluis.com/gastronomia/enchiladas-potosinas/)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Civil-registry pages were not opened in this pass; dates travel with the heritage narrative rather than a scanned acta. Absence of competing dates and consistent secondary citation support treating the claim as true for editorial purposes, with that caveat.

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium — Consistent secondary/heritage chain; no public acta consulted.

### CLAIM 3: Josefina Medellín Jalomo named them in 1965

**CLAIM:** "It was her daughter, Josefina Medellín Jalomo, who gave them the name enchiladas potosinas in 1965."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Multiple national/food outlets (Heraldo de México, Vanguardia quoting family branch Coreño) say Josefina/Josefa Medellín Jalomo designated the name around 1965. Strong local counter-sources (ADECSOL, Quadratin, El Universal San Luis, and 2026 municipal FENAE messaging) credit sister Victoria Medellín Jalomo, sometimes placing the naming at Hidalgo 111 around 1960 or in the 1960s more broadly.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [El Heraldo de México — Josefina, 1965](https://heraldodemexico.com.mx/gastrolab/2019/3/7/te-has-preguntado-como-nacieron-las-enchiladas-potosinas-81101.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Vanguardia — Josefina ~1965 via Coreño Castro](https://vanguardia.com.mx/vida/enchiladas-potosinas-97-anos-de-una-rica-tradicion-culinaria-y-artesanal-COVG3242716)
- **Counter-Evidence:** [ADECSOL — Victoria named them; Hidalgo 111](https://adecsol.org.mx/enchiladas-potosinas-soledenses/)
- **Counter-Evidence:** [Quadratin — Victoria bautizó “enchiladas potosinas,” 1960](https://sanluispotosi.quadratin.com.mx/principal/enchiladas-potosinas-a-punto-de-cumplir-el-centenario/)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Core fact—mid-20th-century naming by a daughter of Jalomo—is solid. Which daughter and whether 1960 vs 1965 remain unresolved family-and-local-history disputes. Presenting Josefina/1965 alone overstates certainty.

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium — Competing credible local vs national retellings.

### CLAIM 4: Chile is kneaded into the masa (not poured on top)

**CLAIM:** "Enchiladas potosinas are not enchiladas in the usual sense: the chile is kneaded INTO the masa, not poured on top."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
This is the defining technical trait versus typical enchiladas (including common “suizas”/“rojas” sauce-over-tortilla styles). Larousse’s Diccionario enciclopédico, Wikipedia, and recipe sources all describe masa colored/seasoned with chile, folded like a quesadilla, comal-cooked, then often fried.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Larousse Cocina — Diccionario: Enchiladas potosinas](https://laroussecocina.mx/palabra/enchiladas-potosinas/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Wikipedia — Enchilada potosina](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enchilada_potosina)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
No credible culinary source contradicts chile-in-masa as the structural differentiator. Heat is generally mild (ancho/colorado/cascabel/guajillo variants), matching the FAQ’s mildness framing.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Canonical culinary definition.

### CLAIM 5: Larousse build — ancho or guajillo, cheese fill, frijoles and cueritos

**CLAIM:** "The canonical build, as culinary reference Larousse Cocina describes it: masa colored with chile ancho or guajillo, filled with fresh cheese, onion and a salsa of the same chile, closed like a quesadilla, cooked on the comal and then fried… traditional sides… frijoles and cueritos."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Larousse’s dictionary entry matches masa colored with chile ancho or colorado (not “guajillo”), filled raw with queso fresco, onion, and salsa of the same chile; closed like quesadillas; comal then fry; onion/crema/queso/lechuga; frijoles as accompaniment. Guajillo is common in modern recipes but is not what that Larousse lemma says. Cueritos are strongly attested as a founding-family serving custom (Casa de la Enchilada / granddaughter interview), not as Larousse’s listed sides.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Larousse Cocina — Enchiladas potosinas (diccionario)](https://laroussecocina.mx/palabra/enchiladas-potosinas/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [El Universal San Luis — frijoles y cueritos at Casa de la Enchilada](https://sanluis.eluniversal.com.mx/mas-de-san-luis/la-casa-de-la-enchilada-el-lugar-historico-que-vio-nacer-al-platillo-insignia-de-san-luis-potosi/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Larousse recipe page uses chile ancho in masa](https://laroussecocina.mx/receta/enchiladas-potosinas/)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Technique and cheese/onion/salsa fill are correctly attributed to Larousse. Bundling “guajillo” and “cueritos” into the Larousse citation blends dictionary text with family/market practice and other recipes. Heritage origin stories often cite chile cascabel; later cocina references prefer ancho/colorado; guajillo appears widely in home recipes—variation, not a single fixed chile.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Direct dictionary comparison.

### CLAIM 6: Casa de la Enchilada — Hidalgo 117, ~80 years, family house

**CLAIM:** "La Casa de la Enchilada — Hidalgo 117, Soledad de Graciano Sánchez, one block from the Jardín Hidalgo… still run by descendants after roughly 80 years in the same spot."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
El Universal San Luis (2023) interviews granddaughter María del Carmen Moreno at Hidalgo 117, one block from Jardín Hidalgo, with a plaque as “La casa de la enchilada,” ~80 years of continuous family tradition after the plaza-selling era moved indoors. Business directories also list Enchiladas Pepita at Miguel Hidalgo 117.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [El Universal San Luis — La casa de la enchilada, Hidalgo 117](https://sanluis.eluniversal.com.mx/mas-de-san-luis/la-casa-de-la-enchilada-el-lugar-historico-que-vio-nacer-al-platillo-insignia-de-san-luis-potosi/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Directorio empresarial — ENCHILADAS PEPITA, Miguel Hidalgo 117](https://www.directorioempresarialmexico.com/empresa/0003671053/ENCHILADAS-PEPITA/)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Address, proximity to Jardín Hidalgo, descendant operation, and ~80-year house tradition match. Note parallel Soledad lineages (e.g., Enchiladas Soledenses / Vicenta Coreño at Matamoros) also claim inheritance—does not falsify Hidalgo 117 as the plaque-marked family house.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — On-site press interview + directory listing.

**CLAIM:** "Rincón Huasteco — Cuauhtémoc 232, Col. Moderna… its Tuesday enchilada festival runs eleven varieties, and the zacahuil is the real thing."

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Official site and menus place the restaurant at Cuauhtémoc 232, Col. Moderna; they advertise a Tuesday Festival de la Enchilada with 11 varieties of enchiladas huastecas and promote traditional zacahuil. Zacahuil quality is subjective (“the real thing”) and not scored; address and festival format are factual.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Rincón Huasteco official site — Festival / 11 variedades](https://www.rinconhuasteco.com/)
- **Corroborating Source:** [carta.menu — Cuauhtémoc 232](https://carta.menu/restaurants/san-luis-potosi/rincon-huasteco)

Venue facts hold. “Best of” / ranking language elsewhere in the article is treated separately (Claim 9). Enchiladas served here are marketed as huastecas varieties, not exclusively “potosinas” of the Soledad masa style—still a valid downtown enchilada destination as the article frames it.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — Self-published venue facts corroborated by aggregators.

**CLAIM:** "Soledad celebrates its dish with the Feria Nacional de la Enchilada (FENAE), a food-and-music fair with 14 years of history held at the Jardín Hidalgo. The 2026 edition ran April 4–12 with free entry; according to the municipal government, it drew over 600,000 visitors."

## PATTERN ANALYSIS

### ACCURACY PATTERNS
Strong on geographic origin (Soledad), structural definition of the dish, restaurant addresses/years, and FENAE 2026 logistics. Weaker where oral family history splits (naming daughter/year) and where Larousse is paraphrased too loosely (chile type; cueritos).

### BIAS INDICATORS
Mild boosterism toward Casa de la Enchilada and selected capital restaurants; competing Soledad vendors (e.g., Enchiladas Soledenses) are omitted—editorial choice, not a falsehood. Two origin legends are presented fairly as competing versions.

### SOURCING QUALITY
Good triangulation for venues and fair; heritage claims rest on decree/municipal narrative plus press, not civil-registry primary documents for 1919/1874–1973.
