## DETAILED FINDINGS

### CLAIM 1: INM San Luis Potosí street address

**CLAIM:** "Address: Av. Venustiano Carranza 1805, Col. Tequisquiapan, San Luis Potosí, S.L.P. 78250"

**VERDICT:** ❌ FALSE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
The official INM Representación in San Luis Potosí is not on Venustiano Carranza. INM’s own office page lists Calle Muñoz No. 362, Fracc. Muñoz 1ª Sección, C.P. 78165. Independent IOM service sheets for SLP (June 2026) repeat Muñoz 362 for immigration trámites. No credible government source places the INM at Carranza 1805 (that corridor hosts other agencies and foreign consular desks, not INM trámites).

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [INM — Oficina de Representación en San Luis Potosí](https://www.inm.gob.mx/gobmx/word/index.php/san-luis-potosi/) — Calle Muñoz No. 362, Fracc. Muñoz 1ª Sección, C.P. 78165
- **Corroborating Source:** [IOM Mexico — Ficha de servicios SLP (June 2026 PDF)](https://mexico.iom.int/sites/g/files/tmzbdl1686/files/documents/2026-09/260825_san-luis_ficha-de-servicios_junio-2026.pdf) — INM trámites at Calle Muñoz No. 362

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Giving readers Carranza 1805 / Tequisquiapan / CP 78250 would send them to the wrong part of the city. The correct federal address for residence-card canje and related trámites is Muñoz 362. Venustiano Carranza addresses in SLP appear for other entities (e.g., foreign consulates and INEGI nearby), which may explain the mix-up, but it is not the INM office.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — contradicted by the institute’s own published office listing and a 2026 IOM directory.

### CLAIM 2: INM SLP hours and phone

**CLAIM:** "Hours: Monday to Friday: 9:00 AM - 1:00 PM… Phone: +52 (444) 814-0814" (also: document reception usually ends at 12:00 PM)

**VERDICT:** ❌ FALSE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
INM’s official SLP page lists public hours Monday–Friday 09:00–15:00 and telephone (444) 833 1959. The IOM June 2026 sheet lists 9:00 am–2:00 pm and phones 444 833 1959 / 444 833 2481. Neither source matches 9:00–1:00, a noon cutoff as a stated rule, or 814-0814.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [INM — Oficina SLP](https://www.inm.gob.mx/gobmx/word/index.php/san-luis-potosi/) — Lunes a Viernes 09:00 a 15:00 hrs; Tel. (444) 833 1959
- **Corroborating Source:** [IOM Mexico — Ficha de servicios SLP (June 2026)](https://mexico.iom.int/sites/g/files/tmzbdl1686/files/documents/2026-09/260825_san-luis_ficha-de-servicios_junio-2026.pdf) — 9:00 am–2:00 pm; 444 833 1959 / 444 833 2481

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Weekday service is correct in broad strokes, but the published closing time and phone number are wrong enough to strand applicants. Treat 9:00–15:00 and 833-1959 as the authoritative published contacts; local practice on last ticket/document intake can still vary day to day, so the article’s “usually ends at 12:00” remains an unsourced local tip, not an official schedule.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — two independent directories disagree with the article’s phone and closing hour.

### CLAIM 3: 30-day post-entry canje deadline

**CLAIM:** "Once you enter Mexico with a pre-approved visa, you have 30 days to complete the process at INM" / "You must complete the second step within 30 calendar days of entry."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
INM lineamientos require that holders of residence (or adoption-visitor) visas be documented with an FMM valid for thirty natural days for canje, and must file the canje trámite within thirty natural days of entry at the INM office for their domicile. Consular guidance repeats the same 30-day rule.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [INM — Lineamientos para trámites y procedimientos migratorios (PDF)](https://www.inm.gob.mx/micrositio/1/docs/mj/Lin_tramites_y_procedimentos.pdf) — FMM for canje: treinta días naturales; file within treinta días naturales of entry
- **Corroborating Source:** [INM — Expedición de documento por canje (requirements PDF)](https://www.inm.gob.mx/static/Tramites/expedicion_documento_canje.pdf) — canje of FMM for visitor/resident card after consular visa

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Calling them “calendar days” tracks “días naturales.” The visa sticker is only entry authorization; the residence card comes from the INM canje. Missing the window risks irregular status and exit/regularization consequences — the article’s urgency here is justified.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — primary INM procedural rules, unchanged in substance for this point.

### CLAIM 4: Temporary and permanent card fees ($4,910 / $5,889 MXN)

**CLAIM:** Cost table lists Temporary Resident Card $4,910 MXN (~$280 USD), Permanent Resident Card $5,889 MXN (~$335 USD), Annual Renewal (Temporary) $4,910; canje fee “approximately $4,000-6,000 MXN (temporary) or $5,000-8,000 MXN (permanent).”

**VERDICT:** 🔄 OUTDATED

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Those peso amounts do not match 2025 full rates (~$5,570 temporary / ~$6,789 permanent) and are far below 2026 statutory amounts after the DOF reform of 7 November 2025 to Ley Federal de Derechos article 8. As of 2026, temporary residence for one year is $11,140.74 MXN and permanent is $13,578.96 MXN (100% cuota), with a 50% reduction only when the applicant proves family unity, employer-registered job offer, or non-remunerated institutional invitation.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Cámara de Diputados — Ley Federal de Derechos (art. 8, reform DOF 07-11-2025)](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LFD.pdf) — Residente Temporal 1 año $11,140.74; Permanente $13,578.96; 50% paragraph for VI/VII
- **Corroborating Source:** [DOF decree excerpt (Diputados Gaceta PDF)](https://gaceta.diputados.gob.mx/PDF/66/2025/oct/DOF07NOV2025-VariosLeyFedDerechos.pdf) — same art. 8 amounts; [Garrigues — INM 2026 fee practice (19 Feb 2026)](https://www.garrigues.com/latam/es_ES/noticia/mexico-inm-ajusta-tarifas-derechos-tramites-migratorios-2026-refuerza-necesidad-acreditar)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Even as a December 2025 “updated” guide, $4,910 / $5,889 were already below contemporaneous 2025 publishing (~$5,570 / ~$6,789). By verification date (21 Sep 2026), readers paying only the article’s amounts would underpay. Economic-solvency and investor applicants typically face the full 100% cuota; the article never mentions the 2026 50%/100% split. Multi-year temporary cards also cost more ($16,693.36 / $21,142.58 / $25,057.82 for 2/3/4 years at 100%).

**CONFIDENCE:** High — statutory LFD figures plus secondary legal commentary on INM application of the 50% rule.

### CLAIM 5: 2025 financial solvency thresholds (~$2,500 / ~$4,200 USD monthly)

**CLAIM:** "Temporary Resident ~$2,500 USD/month or ~$42,000 USD [savings]; Permanent Resident ~$4,200 USD/month or ~$175,000 USD" (labeled Financial Solvency Requirements 2025).

**VERDICT:** 🔄 OUTDATED

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Solvency is set in days of UMA in the visa lineamientos, not fixed USD. A 2025 Mexican Consulate Houston sheet already required ~$4,342 USD/month (or ~$73,189 average monthly balance) for temporary economic solvency, and ~$7,281 USD/month (or ~$292,820 balance) for permanent retirement/pension — roughly double the article’s income figures and far above its savings figures. With UMA 2026 at $117.31/day (INEGI), peso thresholds rose again (~$79,771 MXN/month or ~$1.34M MXN balance for temporary; higher still for permanent pensioners).

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Consulado de México en Houston — Residente Temporal por Solvencia Económica (2025 PDF)](https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/houston/images/Visas/2025/residente-temporal-solvencia-economica-2025.pdf) — 680 días UMA income / 11,460 días UMA balance
- **Corroborating Source:** [Consulado de México en Houston — Residente Permanente jubilación/pensión (2025 PDF)](https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/houston/images/Visas/2025/residente-permanente-jubilacion-o-pensionista-2025.pdf); [INEGI — UMA values](https://www.inegi.org.mx/temas/uma/) — 2025 $113.14 / 2026 $117.31 daily

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
The article correctly notes that amounts track UMA and that consulates should be checked — then publishes USD figures that look like older (pre-2024/2025) conversions. Using ~$2,500 / ~$42,000 as planning numbers in late 2025–2026 would leave applicants under-documented at interview.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — consular requirement sheets plus official UMA series.

### CLAIM 6: Temporary residence 1–4 years; permanente after four consecutive years

**CLAIM:** "Temporary Resident… granted for 1-4 years and can be renewed. After 4 consecutive years, you can apply for permanent residency."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Ley de Migración art. 52 authorizes residente temporal for more than 180 days and up to four years. Art. 54 fraction V grants residente permanente when four years have elapsed since the foreigner held temporary residence permission. INM change-of-status fichas require an explicit request after those four years (it is not automatic).

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Ley de Migración — art. 54](https://mley.mx/LMigra/articulo/54/) (text of DOF law) — cuatro años as residente temporal pathway to permanente
- **Corroborating Source:** [INM — Cambio de condición residente temporal a permanente (PDF)](https://www.inm.gob.mx/static/Tramites/cambio_de_condicion_estancia/Residente_temporal_a_residente_permanente.pdf)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
Card validity may be issued for one, two, three, or four years depending on the authorization, so “1–4 years” is correct. “Consecutive” is a fair practical reading of continuous temporary status, though the legal phrasing is “hayan transcurrido cuatro años desde que… cuenta con un permiso de residencia temporal.” Permanente is also available via other art. 54 routes (family unity, pensioner, points, Mexican children, etc.) without waiting four years.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — statute plus INM trámite ficha.

### CLAIM 7: Permanent residents must enter Mexico at least once every five years

**CLAIM:** "you can remain outside Mexico indefinitely without losing status (though you should enter at least once every 5 years)."

**VERDICT:** ❌ FALSE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Current Ley de Migración authorizes permanente residence indefinitely with the right to enter and leave as desired. The old Ley General de Población absence clocks (including multi-year abroad limits) were repealed with the 2011 migration reform. Art. 64 cancellation grounds do not include a five-year re-entry requirement. The parenthetical advice invents a rule that is not in force.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Ley de Migración — art. 52 / 54 / 64 (Diputados PDF)](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LMigra.pdf) — indefinite permanente stay; cancellation causes listed without a 5-year entry duty
- **Corroborating Source:** [CISVAC analysis of absence rules post-LGP repeal](https://www.cisvac.org/post/residentes-temporales-o-permanentes-en-m%C3%A9xico-cu%C3%A1nto-tiempo-puedo-durar-fuera-del-pa%C3%ADs)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
The first clause (“remain outside… indefinitely”) is closer to the law than the “every 5 years” caveat. That caveat resembles repealed inmigrado absence math or informal expat lore, not current INM/SEGOB rules. Practical advice (keep the card, avoid declaring definitive exit, comply with other art. 64 risks) does not require inventing a five-year visit mandate.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — statutory silence plus documented repeal of the old absence regime.

### CLAIM 8: Tourist FMM up to 180 days

**CLAIM:** "The Forma Migratoria Múltiple (FMM) allows citizens of many countries to enter Mexico without a visa for up to 180 days. However, immigration officers… can grant anywhere from 7 to 180 days at their discretion."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Visitante sin permiso para realizar actividades remuneradas / FMM authorization for visa-exempt nationals is capped at up to 180 natural days under the migration framework; the period actually stamped is set by the immigration officer at the point of entry within that legal maximum. The article’s warning that tourist status does not authorize Mexican remunerated work matches the condition-of-stay design (work permission is a separate track).

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Ley de Migración / INM resumen de condiciones de estancia](https://www.inm.gob.mx/static/marco_juridico/pdf/Ley_de_Migracion_y_Reglamento.pdf) — visitante sin permiso; temporalidades in lineamientos
- **Corroborating Source:** [INM lineamientos — visitante sin permiso vigencia hasta 180 días naturales with visa](https://www.inm.gob.mx/micrositio/1/docs/mj/Lin_tramites_y_procedimentos.pdf)

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
“Many countries” is accurate for visa-exempt nationalities published by SRE; not every nationality is visa-free. Officer discretion below 180 days is a long-standing operational reality and is fairly disclosed.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — legal maximum and operational practice align with the claim’s core.

### CLAIM 9: Temporary residence must be renewed annually for up to four years

**CLAIM:** "Temporary residence: renew annually for up to 4 years, then apply for permanent" / cost table equates “Annual Renewal (Temporary)” with the same fee as first issuance.

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Temporary cards can be authorized for one, two, three, or four years; renewal is required when that authorized period ends, not automatically every year. A one-year card does renew yearly, but multi-year cards do not. Fees for renewal track the temporalidad requested (and, in 2026, 50% vs 100% cuotas), so labeling every renewal “annual” at a single peso amount oversimplifies.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [INM lineamientos — tarjeta de residente temporal vigencia de uno, dos, tres o cuatro años](https://www.inm.gob.mx/micrositio/1/docs/mj/Lin_tramites_y_procedimentos.pdf)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Ley Federal de Derechos art. 8 fracc. VI](https://www.diputados.gob.mx/LeyesBiblio/pdf/LFD.pdf) — separate cuotas by 1/2/3/4-year temporalidad

**DETAILED ANALYSIS:**
The path “temporary up to four years, then permanente” is right; the blanket “renew annually” wording is only true for one-year authorizations. Readers should check the expiration date printed on the card.

**CONFIDENCE:** High — clear mismatch between card temporalidades and the article’s annual-only framing.

## PATTERN ANALYSIS

### ACCURACY PATTERNS
Process architecture (consulate → entry → 30-day INM canje; visa categories; FMM 180-day cap; four-year bridge to permanente) is largely sound. Concrete local logistics and money figures—the SLP office pin, phone, published fees, and USD solvency estimates—are where the piece fails hardest.

### BIAS INDICATORS
Tone is pro-expat and service-oriented rather than partisan. Risk of harm comes from outdated “actionable” numbers and a wrong office address, not from agenda-driven framing. The “Verified and Updated December 2025 / 15+ Official Sources” badge overstates currency relative to DOF/INM primary data as of September 2026.

### SOURCING QUALITY
The article cites INM/SEGOB conceptually but does not reproduce current LFD art. 8 amounts, the Muñoz 362 office listing, or UMA-day solvency formulas. Primary-source depth in this investigation was strong (INM, DOF/LFD, SRE consular PDFs, INEGI UMA).
