## DETAILED FINDINGS

### CLAIM 1: Xantolo's core days are October 31 to November 2

**CLAIM:** "Xantolo is the Huasteca's Day of the Dead — celebrated October 31 to November 2."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
UNESCO's feature on the Teenek Xantolo places it between 31 October and 2 November, with huehues dancing from 1 November. Some sources and 2026 programmes start the public activities on 30 October, which is consistent with a core of 31 October–2 November.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [UNESCO, Xantolo, la fiesta teenek que arropa a los antepasados](https://www.unesco.org/es/articles/xantolo-la-fiesta-teenek-que-arropa-los-antepasados-y-quienes-vienen-de-paso)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Plano Informativo, proposal for an Artisan Mask-Maker Day (Xantolo 30 Oct–2 Nov)](https://planoinformativo.com/1174745/proponen-declarar-el-23-de-octubre-el-dia-del-artesano-mascarero)

**CONFIDENCE:** High.

### CLAIM 2: The name comes from *sanctorum*, filtered through Nahuatl

**CLAIM:** "The name comes from sanctorum, filtered through Nahuatl."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
México Desconocido and Crónica both derive Xantolo from Nahuatl *Xantolon*, which in turn comes from Latin *sanctorum* (*Festum omnium sanctorum*). An academic article notes an alternative theory (Nahuatlised Spanish "santo/todos"), but the *sanctorum* route is the most widely cited.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Dialnet, academic article on Xantolo etymology](https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/6734639.pdf)
- **Corroborating Source:** [México Desconocido, Xantolo](https://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/xantolo-la-festividad-de-dia-de-muertos-de-la-huasteca-potosina.html); [Crónica, ¿Qué es el Xantolo?](https://www.cronica.com.mx/cultura/2024/10/29/que-es-el-xantolo-la-fiesta-de-todas-las-animas-de-origen-prehispanico/)

**CONFIDENCE:** High.

### CLAIM 3: The ritual cycle opens September 29 (San Miguel Arcángel) and closes November 30

**CLAIM:** "The ritual cycle opens as early as September 29 (San Miguel Arcángel) and closes with a final offering on November 30."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Universidad Veracruzana and UNAM-cited sources describe a small offering on 29 September for San Miguel Arcángel. UAEH research describes 30 November (San Andrés) as the farewell to the dead, with the offering in the corn cribs. México Desconocido covered the first offering of the season in honour of Miguel Arcángel.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [UAEH Gaceta, El Xantolo en la Huasteca potosina](https://uaeh.edu.mx/gaceta/3/numero33/noviembre/xantolo-huasteca.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Universidad Veracruzana, Universo (Nov 2015)](https://uv.mx/universo/?p=11038); [México Desconocido, first Xantolo offering for Miguel Arcángel](https://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/colocan-la-primer-ofrenda-del-xantolo-2025-en-honor-a-miguel-arcangel.html)

**CONFIDENCE:** High.

### CLAIM 4: Dancing masked is said to confuse death itself so it can't take the living

**CLAIM:** "The carved wooden masks aren't costume for its own sake: dancing masked is said to confuse death itself, so it can't take the living."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
UNESCO's account says huehues wear wooden masks so the dead don't recognise them and so they avoid direct contact with the souls. Other sources describe the comanche-style dances as a struggle between life and death. The "confuse death" reading is one popular variant, but it should not be the only explanation given. The passage now leads with the documented meaning and keeps the popular version as "some say".

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [UNESCO, Xantolo teenek feature](https://www.unesco.org/es/articles/xantolo-la-fiesta-teenek-que-arropa-los-antepasados-y-quienes-vienen-de-paso)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Criterio Hidalgo, comparsas del Xantolo](https://criteriohidalgo.com/noticias/hidalgo/asi-se-vive-el-xantolo-en-la-huasteca-comparsas-que-entrelazan-el-pasado-indigena-con-el-legado-colonial)

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium. This is folklore with several local variants.

### CLAIM 5: UNESCO inscribed Mexico's indigenous festivity dedicated to the dead in 2008

**CLAIM:** "UNESCO documents the tradition as part of Mexico's indigenous festivities dedicated to the dead, inscribed on the Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2008."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
"Indigenous festivity dedicated to the dead" (Mexico) was proclaimed a Masterpiece in 2003 and incorporated into the Representative List in 2008. The post correctly frames Xantolo as part of that element rather than as a separate inscription. UNESCO itself has published a feature on the Teenek Xantolo.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [UNESCO ICH, Indigenous festivity dedicated to the dead](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/indigenous-festivity-dedicated-to-the-dead-00054)
- **Corroborating Source:** [UNESCO, Xantolo teenek feature](https://www.unesco.org/es/articles/xantolo-la-fiesta-teenek-que-arropa-los-antepasados-y-quienes-vienen-de-paso)

**CONFIDENCE:** High.

### CLAIM 6: San Luis Potosí declared Xantolo state cultural patrimony in 2013

**CLAIM:** "San Luis Potosí declared it state cultural patrimony in 2013."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
The SLP Congress approved the declaration of the indigenous Day of the Dead festivity (Xantolo) as intangible state heritage in a session held in Tamazunchale on 7 November 2013, after a technical opinion from the state Culture Secretariat.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Plano Informativo, Desde 2013, Xantolo es patrimonio cultural de SLP](https://planoinformativo.com/355830/desde-2013-xantolo-es-patrimonio-cultural-de-slp)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Potosinoticias, Since 2013 Xantolo is state heritage](https://potosinoticias.com/2021/10/13/desde-2013-xantolo-es-patrimonio-cultural-del-estado-investigadora/); [Agenda San Luis, Xantolo declared intangible heritage](https://agendasanluis.com/xantolo-fue-declarado-patrimonio-cultural-intangible-de-san-luis-potosi/)

**CONFIDENCE:** High.

### CLAIM 7: Xantolo is strongest along the "so-called Ruta Xantolo"

**CLAIM:** "Xantolo is strongest along the so-called Ruta Xantolo: Tamazunchale, Axtla de Terrazas, Xilitla, Aquismón, Tancanhuitz, Ciudad Valles and a dozen more Huasteca municipalities."

**VERDICT:** ❓ UNVERIFIABLE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
The listed municipalities are well-documented Xantolo centres, and 2026 coverage says more than 15 Huasteca municipalities host activities. No official or media source uses "Ruta Xantolo" for this list. The label was dropped and the towns kept.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- No source found using "Ruta Xantolo" for a defined route. Searches of state tourism and media coverage returned none.

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium.

### CLAIM 8: Comparsa characters include the "Comanche"

**CLAIM:** "Every town has its own flavor — mask styles, dance characters like the 'Comanche'…"

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Comanche figures carrying whips appear in Huasteca Xantolo comparsas and dances, and Xantolo "La Comanche" masks are catalogued by mask collections.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Mask Museum, Xantolo La Comanche mask](https://www.maskmuseum.org/?p=11074)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Crónica, ¿Qué es el Xantolo?](https://www.cronica.com.mx/cultura/2024/10/29/que-es-el-xantolo-la-fiesta-de-todas-las-animas-de-origen-prehispanico/)

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium.

### CLAIM 9: Zacahuil can stretch past a meter and weigh 20–50 kg, wrapped in banana or papatla leaves and cooked overnight in a wood-fired oven

**CLAIM:** "Zacahuil — a ceremonial tamal that can stretch past a meter and weigh 20–50 kg… wrapped in banana or papatla leaves and cooked overnight in a wood-fired oven."

**VERDICT:** ✅ TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Sources give 1–1.5 m as typical and up to 5 m, weights from about 20 kg to 60 kg, papatla or banana leaves, and 8–12 hours in a wood-fired pit oven. UNESCO notes that even the smallest are 70–90 cm.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [México Desconocido, Zacahuil](https://www.mexicodesconocido.com.mx/zacahuil.html)
- **Corroborating Source:** [UNAM Global, el tamal gigante de la Huasteca](https://unamglobal.unam.mx/global_tv/zacahuil-el-tamal-gigante-que-es-esencia-misma-de-la-huasteca/); [Criterio Hidalgo, tamal más grande de México](https://criteriohidalgo.com/noticias/hidalgo/este-es-el-tamal-grande-de-mexico-que-se-consume-en-la-huasteca)

**CONFIDENCE:** High.

### CLAIM 10: Around zacahuil: bolín and chilpan tamales

**CLAIM:** "Around it: bolín, chilpan tamales, pan de muerto shared at the altar, café de olla and atole."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
The bolim (bolín) is well documented as a Xantolo offering tamal. UNESCO describes it as chicken-sized, and a medical-anthropology article lists it in Teenek and Nahua offerings. No source attests a "chilpan" tamal tied to Xantolo. Replaced with "bolim (a smaller, chicken-sized tamal), other tamales".

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [UNESCO, Xantolo teenek feature (bolín)](https://www.unesco.org/es/articles/xantolo-la-fiesta-teenek-que-arropa-los-antepasados-y-quienes-vienen-de-paso)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Medigraphic, Xantolo offerings (bolín)](https://www.medigraphic.com/pdfs/plasticidad/prn-2021/prn212g.pdf)

**CONFIDENCE:** Medium.

### CLAIM 11: The Huasteca towns are 4–5 hours from SLP capital by road

**CLAIM:** "Getting there: the Huasteca towns are 4–5 hours from SLP capital by road; Ciudad Valles is the region's hub."

**VERDICT:** ⚠️ PARTIALLY TRUE

**INVESTIGATION SUMMARY:**
Ciudad Valles is about 265 km away: about 3 hours by car and 3 h 15 min to 4 h 30 min by bus. Tamazunchale is about 5 hours away. "4–5 hours" overstates the trip to the main hub. Changed to "roughly 3.5–5 hours", with Valles at about 3.5 hours.

**EVIDENCE CHAIN:**
- **Primary Source:** [Travel and Time, Ciudad Valles to San Luis Potosí driving time](https://travelandtime.com/driving-time-from-ciudad-valles-to-san-luis-potosi)
- **Corroborating Source:** [Bookaway, SLP to Ciudad Valles](https://www.bookaway.com/routes/mexico/san-luis-potosi-to-ciudad-valles); [Posta, Tamazunchale 5 hours from the capital](https://www.posta.com.mx/share/san-luis-potosi-tiene-un-paraiso-oculto-y-esta-a-5-horas-de-la-capital/vl2025788)

**CONFIDENCE:** High.
