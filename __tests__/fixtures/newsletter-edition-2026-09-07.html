📰 Top News

La Campiña threatens water blockade

Villa de Pozos residents may block Camino a Santa Rita unless authorities restore reliable water service across La Campiña.

Why it matters: A blockade would disrupt commuters while thousands of households already depend on tanker deliveries.

  • At least 15 Villa de Pozos neighborhoods report water shortages.
  • The municipality distributes roughly 700,000 liters daily through water tankers.

Go deeper: vía Pulso SLP

2. Passenger train eyes Red Metro

Transport planners want the future passenger railway connected with Red Metro, creating a transfer point between regional trains and our city’s bus network.

Why it matters: A direct transfer could reduce car trips and simplify arrivals from other cities.

3. Centro markets get refresh

City planners are preparing maintenance at Mercado Hidalgo, Mercado República and La Merced, beginning with paint and building-condition reviews.

Why it matters: The work could temporarily shift entrances, stalls and delivery access for centro shoppers.

⚡ Quick Hits

  • Parque Morales: Crews will remove 30 trees affected by pests or structural damage.
  • Testaments: September’s discount campaign can save residents up to $6,410 MXN on the legal process.
  • Las Vías: Sunday cleanup begins as vendors leave Avenida Hernán Cortés, reopening the road after the tianguis.

🙋 Ask an Expat

Q: "How do I get an RFC as a foreign resident?"

Book “Inscripción al RFC” at the SAT appointment portal, then bring your passport, valid residency card, CURP and Mexican address proof. For help, call MarcaSAT at +52 55 627 2272.

💡 Expat Pro Tip

Pay centro parking by phone

Install the “Gobierno Municipal SLP” app before parking in el centro. Start or extend your parquímetro session without hunting for coins or returning to the meter.

🌿 Weekend Escape

Sótano de las Huahuas

Hundreds of green parakeets and white-collared swifts spiral from this 478-meter Huasteca chasm at sunrise and return near sunset.

  • Why go: September’s green landscape adds drama, but wet trails get slippery; hire a local guide and skip the descent after heavy rain.
  • The details: About five hours from SLP; entry is approximately $60 MXN, plus roughly $594 MXN in round-trip tolls.

🧠 Did You Know?

SLP once reached Texas

The colonial Intendancy of San Luis Potosí included Nuevo León, Tamaulipas, Coahuila and Texas. Our landlocked state once governed territory reaching the Gulf Coast.
